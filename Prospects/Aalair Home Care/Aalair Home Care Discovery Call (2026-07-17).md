## What Actually Came Out on the Call

### The positioning pivot (this is the real ask, not just a redesign)

Aalair was founded caregiver-focused — "are you caring for your parent already? You can get paid for it" — a Medicaid-program-driven model. That model is losing viability as government benefit rules shift, so Aalair is pivoting hard to **private-pay, client-centered, concierge-style home care** (explicitly modeled on Home Instead). The current site still reads Medicaid/caregiver-focused, and it's now actively hurting sales: *"I have literally seen people's faces change as I'm talking to them when they look at our brochures or our website... they're there because they need help... it doesn't check with what we're doing."* Urgency driver: they just hired a full-time salesperson, and the mismatch is costing conversions in real time.

Target positioning: "concierge home care agency," peace-of-mind partnership, "what can I do to get to yes," dignity/reliability/stability — Tamra's own founder story (cared for her mother while raising her own kids, "sandwich generation") is a strong trust-narrative asset, currently unused on the site.

### Audience correction

Actual customer base skews **Caucasian, ages 70–90** — current site imagery doesn't reflect that and she wants it corrected, explicitly framed as accuracy/representation, not the reverse.

### Visual/brand direction

- Drop "Love" from "Integrity, Love, Support & Community" — "a little much"
- Move away from red/bold color (perceives it as "aggressive," "induces hunger" per something she'd read) toward **softer, pastel tones** — cited Father's Heart as the tonal reference, while also flagging its readability problem as something to avoid replicating
- Wants a professional copywriter's help with verbiage: *"I need a marketing professional to help me with that... if I can find somebody to create that messaging for me, that would be great."*

### Site structure / functional asks

- De-emphasize the "apply to be a caregiver" recruitment CTA — move it off the prominent/top spot to a secondary position; center the *client* (person seeking care), not the caregiver
- Google reviews widget, with last names stripped for privacy; actively collecting testimonials from clients (~2 months in), goal ~80
- Contact form: route straight to email, ask more qualifying detail (when do you need services to start, situation context) — balanced against Elyse's caution not to over-ask and scare prospects off. Bob flagged a clear "what happens next" confirmation as important so submissions don't feel like a black hole
- HIPAA/insurance-adjacent form handling — Bob flagged as a known, previously-solved constraint, not a blocker
- Services dropdown/mega-menu preferred over horizontal-scroll nav
- Blog interest mentioned in passing ("we thought about the blog")

### SEO / AEO

Current site is missing basic on-page SEO — several pages have no real H1 (cursive-script header images instead of text), no FAQ content despite competitors having it on nearly every page. Tamra was separately pitched by another agency on ChatGPT/AI-search visibility and asked directly whether MWFM can help there. Elyse's answer: AI/LLM visibility rides on the same fundamentals (site structure, authority, UX) as traditional SEO — a new build ships SEO-ready (keyword-informed content from the content/SEO team), but sustained AI/search visibility needs an **ongoing SEO service**, not a one-time build. Bob's recommended build approach: launch with a strong core + single services page, then build out individual service pages progressively using real post-launch search data (he cited dementia/Alzheimer's care as an example of a term already showing volume) rather than front-loading every page speculatively — this naturally sets up an ongoing content/SEO retainer.

### Hosting

Current site is on GoDaddy — both Bob and a separate agency Tamra consulted independently called it bottom-tier/poor-performing, with plugins not kept current. Tamra is already decided on moving off it: *"So, we won't use it."* Explicitly open to MWFM hosting + maintenance if the build goes through. No technical blocker to migrating.

# R&V Cleaning Solutions — Voice

Read by every content/copy skill before drafting for this client, alongside [[R&V Cleaning Solutions Brand]].

## Format & Style Defaults

Agency-wide baseline — every client starts here. Override or add to any of these in Brand-Specific Rules below if this client needs something different; don't delete this section to do it.

**Tone:** Simple and authentic.

**Reading Level:** Below 8th grade. Aim for 6th or 7th.

**Avoid:**
- Cringe or hype language
- Sales language
- Exclamation points
- Colons used as sentence separators
- Repeating yourself
- Em dashes as separators or for emphasis. Use a period or comma instead.
- Hyphens as separators. Use a period or comma instead.
- Years in claims (like "best XXX of 2026") unless the year is actually needed, like news. It ages badly.

**Dashes — when they ARE allowed:**
- Ranges and comparisons: $2-$4, 30-60 minutes, ages 25-40
- Compound/hyphenated words: pre-filled, well-known, long-term, evidence-based

**Text Format:**
- Prefer short sentences and short paragraphs with room to breathe. Vary the length, don't lock into one rhythm.
- Conversational, human language, not corporate copy.
- Use lists only when a list actually helps. Don't reach for one by default.
- Use H2 and H3 for major sections.
- No orphaned headings. If a paragraph doesn't follow a heading, cut the heading.
- Mix in some longer sentences with conjunctions and subordinate clauses, but keep short ones as the backbone.
- Define new terms when introduced.
- Vary the mood throughout the writing.

**Client-specific format notes (fill in during onboarding):**
- **Sentence length:** Short, tagline-style for headlines and hero copy ("We ease your cleaning hassles," "Quality Work, Excellent Value"). No long-form body copy exists yet to judge paragraph rhythm against — Catherine's Feb 2026 feedback confirmed body copy for Our Process/FAQ/footer was never written.
- **Punctuation habits:** No colons, no exclamation points observed across any client-provided asset — consistent with the agency default, not a deviation.
- **Structural habits (lists vs. prose):** Service lists are genuinely bulleted/tagged (Deep Cleaning, Regular Maintenance, Move-In/Move-Out Cleaning) rather than written into prose — keep that pattern for service menus specifically.
- **Headline style:** Short benefit statements, not questions ("Quality Work, Excellent Value," "More than clean - it's a fresh start!")
- **What this brand avoids, beyond the defaults above:** Nothing additional observed yet — will need real client copy samples to confirm rather than DIY-site inference alone.

## Brand-Specific Rules

**Brand voice:** Warm, plainspoken, reassuring — a small local business leading with trust and value, not polish.

**This brand sounds like:** A friendly neighborhood cleaning company that talks the way it would to a customer on the phone — short, direct, no jargon, no hard sell.

**Preferred terminology:**

| Say | Not |
|-----|-----|
| Deep Cleaning | Deep Clean(ing) service (variant casing) |
| Regular Maintenance | Recurring cleaning |
| Move-In/Move-Out Cleaning | Move in / move out clean |

**Additional rules observed from site copy / client feedback:**

- No customer reviews or testimonials exist anywhere yet — don't fabricate or imply any until real ones are collected (Catherine flagged this as an open gap in Feb 2026, still unresolved).
- Treat "+10 years of experience | +500 happy clients" (current GoDaddy site) as an unverified client claim, not agency-confirmed copy — confirm with Diana before reusing.
- Structure the service pages around three practical categories — Residential, Commercial, Student — since that's what was actually scoped and paid for; don't quietly fold Student into Commercial the way the current DIY site does without confirming that's still what Diana wants.

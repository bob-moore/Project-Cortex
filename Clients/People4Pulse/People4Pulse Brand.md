## 2. Target Audience

- **Primary audience:** Two overlapping groups — (1) people who use drugs and their families/loved ones, seeking harm-reduction resources, community, and a nonjudgmental space; (2) advocates, healthcare providers, and policymakers the org is trying to influence toward evidence-based drug policy. Explicitly Wisconsin-based ("from Kenosha to Superior") but the Declaration/Resources content has broader applicability.
- **Pain points:** Stigma and criminalization that block access to healthcare and support; isolation; loved ones stuck between "enabling" and total-abstinence framing with no middle ground; not knowing overdose-response basics (e.g. Narcan vs. naloxone); distrust of systems that have historically punished rather than helped.
- **Sophistication level:** Mixed by design. Public-facing pages (home, about, resources) are written for general audiences including people newly encountering harm reduction ("New to harm reduction? You're welcome here, everyone is"). Blog content assumes more context and cites sources (SAMHSA, National Harm Reduction Coalition) — written for a slightly more engaged/advocacy-minded reader.
- **Audience language (VOC):** Testimonial quotes on home/about pages are the clearest VOC source: "PULSE gave me a place to be honest about my life without being judged." / "The meetings changed how I understand harm reduction — it's not enabling, it's dignity in practice." / "I didn't think anyone was fighting for people like me." Recurring themes: being heard without judgment, dignity over punishment, community over isolation.

## 3. Brand Voice & Tone

Full rules in [[People4Pulse Voice]]. Summary:

- **Voice in 3–5 words:** Dignified, direct, unflinching, warm
- **This brand sounds like:** An advocate speaking plainly about a life-or-death issue — evidence-based, unapologetic about its politics, but warm and welcoming to newcomers
- **This brand never sounds like:** Clinical/detached public-health jargon without translation, moralizing recovery-industry language ("clean," "addict," "enabling"), or a corporate nonprofit softening its politics for palatability
- **Formality:** Mixed — declarative, confident statements on core pages ("Harm reduction is healthcare. Community is prevention. Autonomy is essential.") alongside warm, second-person welcome copy ("You're welcome here, everyone is"). Blog posts are more explanatory/essayistic, citing sources.
- **Sentence style:** Short, punchy declaratives for taglines and section headers; longer explanatory paragraphs in blog/education content that unpack a single reframe per piece (e.g. abstinence vs. stability, recovery as non-linear)
- **Sample approved copy:** *"Harm reduction is healthcare. Community is prevention. Autonomy is essential."* / *"Meaningful change happens when people are treated with dignity instead of punishment."* / *"Real support isn't about monitoring someone's sobriety; it's about investing in their humanity."*
- **Sample rejected copy:** Anything using "addict," "clean/dirty" (re: drug tests or sobriety), "abuse" (vs. "use"), or framing that treats abstinence as the only valid recovery outcome — directly contradicted by the "Our Thoughts" and "Stability Over Sobriety" content

## 7. Compliance, Legal & Claims

> [!danger] The overdose crisis is framed as bipartisan, not partisan — client-approved rule
> Direct instruction from the 2026-07-07 client build meeting: the "Overdose Crisis" section was explicitly reframed away from "rising deaths statewide" toward **"overdose deaths remain unacceptably high in Wisconsin"** rather than "the overdose deaths have increased across WI." The reasoning, in the client's own words (recording, 2026-07-07): *"This doesn't affect one party and not another. Republicans and Democrats alike know someone who has substance use issues, and they all know somebody who is either in jail or in the ground as a result of failed drug policy."* This is a deliberate, approved positioning choice, not a watering-down — PULSE wants the crisis framed as a shared public-health problem everyone should care about, not a political wedge. Apply this to any new copy touching overdose statistics or the crisis broadly, not just the one section it was called out on.

> [!warning] Anonymity is the default for community testimonials — a separate, named-quote section is different
> Section "Who We Are" in About explicitly states: *"we value the right of individuals to maintain anonymity... unless specified otherwise, all members are anonymous, and we never share names."* Any case studies or community-voice content must default to anonymous/non-identifying attribution (as the current testimonials already do: "Community member · Milwaukee, WI") unless a person has explicitly opted to be named. **Resolved 2026-07-15 from the full meeting recording:** the "3 testimonial quotes with attribution and a source link" Alan is providing are specifically **press/publication quotes from people already publicly on record** — Alan himself has been quoted in the Wisconsin State Journal ("the Sentinel") and elsewhere, PULSE participant Dimmitri has said similar things but hasn't done a formal interview yet, and staff writer Greta may also be quotable from her published writing. This is a distinct "in the press" section, not a change to the community-member anonymity default — don't conflate the two when drafting either section.

- Community Agreements explicitly state law enforcement and news media are not welcome in PULSE spaces unless specifically invited — relevant if any content work involves press outreach or media relations.
- Nonprofit — donation copy should avoid guarantee-of-outcome language; standard nonprofit solicitation practice, no other compliance flags found on-site.
- No explicit 501(c)(3)/501(c)(4) designation stated on site — [TO CONFIRM], relevant for donation-page tax-deductibility language if any is added.
- **Photo consent, case by case:** at least one candidate homepage/events photo includes named individuals (Dr. Elizabeth Salbury, opioid settlement committee; Joseph Gaye, co-chair of the Dane County Opioid Settlement Committee; a Department of Health staffer named Heidi) who may not want to be publicly pictured on an advocacy site given their day jobs — Alan is checking with all three before that photo is used. Treat any photo with identifiable non-member professionals the same way: confirm before publishing.
- **Representation in imagery is a stated value, not incidental:** Alan, in the recording: *"It's really important to me that substance use doesn't take on just a black or brown face — because that's not the truth of substance use, and it's actually been used to stigmatize and harm the people who use drugs in that way."* Apply this when selecting or requesting photos — see [[People4Pulse Voice]].

# People4Pulse — Voice

Read by every content/copy skill before drafting for this client, alongside [[People4Pulse Brand]].

## Format & Style Defaults

Agency-wide baseline (see `templates/Client Voice.md`). **One documented exception for this client** — see Client-Specific Format Notes below: em dashes are used for appositive asides in essay/blog content. This is a real, observed pattern, not an oversight — it overrides the default in that specific context only.

**Tone:** Simple and authentic.

**Reading Level:** Below 8th grade. Aim for 6th or 7th.

**Avoid:**
- Cringe or hype language
- Sales language
- Exclamation points
- Colons used as sentence separators
- Repeating yourself
- Em dashes as separators or for emphasis, outside the appositive-aside exception noted below. Use a period or comma instead.
- Hyphens as separators. Use a period or comma instead.
- Years in claims (like "best XXX of 2026") unless the year is actually needed, like news. It ages badly.

**Dashes — when they ARE allowed:**
- Ranges and comparisons: $2-$4, 30-60 minutes, ages 25-40
- Compound/hyphenated words: pre-filled, well-known, long-term, evidence-based

**Text Format:**
- Prefer short sentences and short paragraphs with room to breathe. Vary the length, don't lock into one rhythm.
- Conversational, human language, not corporate copy.
- Use lists only when a list actually helps. Don't reach for one by default.
- Use H2 and H3 for major sections.
- No orphaned headings. If a paragraph doesn't follow a heading, cut the heading.
- Mix in some longer sentences with conjunctions and subordinate clauses, but keep short ones as the backbone.
- Define new terms when introduced.
- Vary the mood throughout the writing.

## Client-Specific Format Notes

- **Sentence length:** Mixed intentionally — short declarative statements for taglines/headers ("Harm reduction is healthcare."), longer unpacking paragraphs in blog/essay content that build one argument at a time
- **Punctuation habits:** Em dashes used for appositive asides ("...a collaborative group involved in both mutual aid and advocacy...") — an intentional exception to the agency default; quotation marks used heavily to spotlight community-voice lines as pull-quotes
- **Structural habits (lists vs. prose):** Numbered/icon lists for pillars and values (What We Do's 4 pillars, About's 8 values); prose paragraphs for advocacy essays (Our Thoughts, blog); testimonials always short (1–2 sentences) and attributed by role + city, never full name
- **Headline style:** Short, confident, often two-line stacked statements ("Fighting For / Liberty." / "Demanding / Autonomy.") — benefit/mission-first, not question-based
- **What this brand avoids:** Hedging language, apologetic framing, clinical distancing from the audience it serves, exclamation points (none observed in body copy despite the advocacy energy — confidence reads through word choice, not punctuation)

## Brand-Specific Rules

**Brand voice:** Dignified, direct, unflinching, warm

**This brand sounds like:** An advocate speaking plainly about a life-or-death issue — evidence-based, unapologetic about its politics, but warm and welcoming to people encountering harm reduction for the first time.

**Preferred terminology:**

| Say | Not |
|-----|-----|
| people who use drugs / PWUD | addicts, drug abusers |
| harm reduction | drug abuse treatment (as if the only frame) |
| substance use | drug abuse, substance abuse |
| recovery (self-directed, non-linear, plural pathways) | sobriety as the only valid outcome, "clean" |
| lived experience / living experience | — |
| naloxone (specific product/form) | "Narcan" used as a generic catch-all |

**Additional rules observed from site copy:**

- **Frame the overdose crisis as bipartisan, not partisan** — direct, explicit client instruction from the 2026-07-07 build meeting, applied when reworking the "Overdose Crisis" section: lead with "overdose deaths remain unacceptably high in Wisconsin" rather than "rising deaths statewide," and describe opioid usage as a shared, bipartisan public-health problem, not a political one. Apply this framing to any copy touching crisis statistics, not just the one section it was called out on. See [[People4Pulse Brand]] Section 7.
- Default to anonymous or role+city attribution for any community-voice/testimonial content ("Community member · Milwaukee, WI"), never a full name, unless a person has explicitly consented to be named — this is a stated organizational value (see [[People4Pulse Brand]] Section 7), not just a style preference. **The one exception is a distinct, separate "in the press" quote section** (3 named quotes with source links, from people already publicly on record — Alan, possibly participant Dimmitri, possibly staff writer Greta) — that's a different content type from community testimonials, not a change to the anonymity default itself. Don't blend the two.
- **Imagery should reflect that substance use isn't only a Black or brown issue.** Direct client value, in Alan's words: *"that's not the truth of substance use, and it's actually been used to stigmatize and harm the people who use drugs in that way."* Apply when selecting or requesting photos, not just when writing copy.
- **Photos can and should show real joy, not just advocacy solemnity.** Alan, on selecting event photos: *"community engagement needs to be fun, and this work particularly needs to involve smiles because it inevitably involves so many tears."* Balance harm-reduction education content with genuine community/event photos — don't default to only somber or clinical imagery.
- Present recovery and abstinence as a spectrum of equally valid, self-directed paths — never write copy that implies total abstinence is the goal or that any other outcome is a partial failure. This is a core, repeated editorial position across About, Our Thoughts, and the blog.
- Cite sources when making clinical/public-health claims (SAMHSA, National Harm Reduction Coalition, Partnership to End Addiction are the sources already used) — the blog's authority comes from evidence-based framing, not just advocacy tone.
- Keep the "everyone is welcome" invitation explicit in any meeting/event/get-involved copy — the site repeats variations of "New to harm reduction? You're welcome here, everyone is" as a recurring reassurance for newcomers.
- Law enforcement and news media are not the audience for community-facing copy (per Community Agreements) — draft press/media content, if ever requested, as a distinct track from community-facing content, and flag it for stakeholder review before publishing.
- **Meeting cadence in copy:** weekly membership meetings plus a general membership meeting on the 4th Wednesday of the month (64/year) — not "2nd Tuesday," which was an earlier site-copy misread. Update any new meeting/event copy to match.

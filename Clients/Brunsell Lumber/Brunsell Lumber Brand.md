## 2. Target Audience

Two distinct readers, addressed on the same pages:

- **Contractors and builders** who already know the trade. They want account terms: volume pricing, free delivery, a dedicated account manager, a straightforward way to place standing orders. They don't need the product explained to them.
- **Homeowners and DIY end users**, often planning a single project (deck, mantel, custom millwork, a kitchen or mudroom upgrade). They need the product and process explained in plain terms, and they're comparing Brunsell against a big box store or a competitor like Marling Lumber.

One meeting note framed the writing test directly: copy should work for "a guy that has worked construction his whole life and someone that never has." Neither audience should feel talked down to or lost.

- **Pain points:** Homeowners aren't sure what's possible, what it costs, or how long a custom piece takes. Contractors want reliability and account terms more than education.
- **Sophistication level:** Mixed by design, plain language throughout, but don't strip out real product terms (wood species, finish names) that a homeowner can look up or ask about.

## 3. Brand Voice & Tone

Full rules in [[Brunsell Lumber Voice]]. Summary:

- **Voice in 3-5 words:** Plain, honest, counter-level knowledgeable
- **This brand sounds like:** Someone who works at the yard and knows the product, explaining a project to a customer without a script
- **This brand never sounds like:** The current site's corporate/hype voice, phrases like "talented artisans," "Old World Quality and Craftsmanship," "superior materials," "masterfully crafted"
- **Formality:** Casual-professional, contractions fine, second person ("you") for homeowner content, direct address for contractor content
- **Sentence style:** Short sentences as the backbone, some longer ones with conjunctions, no em dashes, no exclamation points
- **Sample rejected copy (current live site):** "Brunsell Custom Millwork equals unlimited choices and spectacular results!"

## 7. Compliance, Legal & Claims

> [!danger] No installation language, anywhere
> Brunsell does not install. Not materials, not millwork, not decks, doors, or stairways. This has come up repeatedly in meeting notes, so check every draft, page, and CTA for "professional installation," "we install," "our installers," or similar before it ships. See [[Gotchas]].

- Do not imply Brunsell performs installation. They supply materials and can connect customers to a contractor from their list, they don't install.
- Do not say "we design" or "fits perfectly" for millwork and stairways. The team builds to a customer's or contractor's drawing, it does not do structural engineering or design from scratch.
- Design services are free. Avoid any language that implies a design fee.
- Avoid overpromising on natural, variable materials. Never claim a "superior" or "precision" grain match, wood varies piece to piece. Say the wood is individually selected instead.
- Keep "years in business" static and consistent across every page. Don't let one page say 88 years and another say a different number.

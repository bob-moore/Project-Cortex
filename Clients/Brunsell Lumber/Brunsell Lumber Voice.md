# Brunsell Lumber — Voice

Read by every content/copy skill before drafting for this client, alongside [[Brunsell Lumber Brand]]. These rules take precedence over generic style guidance. Built from the client's own format rules, meeting notes with Logan Kosbau's team (March through June 2026), and a review of the current live site (brunsell.com) against the staging rebuild (brunsell.mdmserver.us).

> [!danger] Brunsell does not install
> Never write copy that implies Brunsell installs anything: materials, millwork, decks, doors, or stairways. They supply and build to order. Installation is done by the customer or by a contractor from Brunsell's referral list, not by Brunsell. Watch for "professional installation," "we install," "our installers," "installed by our team," and similar phrasing sneaking into product pages, FAQs, and CTAs. Flagged repeatedly across meeting notes, treat every draft as a chance to slip this back in. See [[Gotchas]].

## Brand Angle

Brunsell has supplied lumber and millwork to the Madison, Wisconsin area since 1938 (88 years as of 2026), family owned, with a second location for components in Mt Horeb. The site has to speak to two different readers at once: contractors and builders who already know the trade, and homeowners who don't. One meeting note put it well: write for "a guy that has worked construction his whole life and someone that never has."

What earns trust with both: plain talk about what Brunsell actually does (supplies materials, builds custom millwork, connects you to contractors) and what it doesn't do (install). What loses it: hype, vague promises, and language that oversells natural materials that vary piece to piece.

## Format & Style Defaults

Agency-wide baseline (see `templates/Client Voice.md`), with this client's own counter-voice framing on Tone.

**Tone:** Simple. Authentic. Write like someone who works at the counter and knows the product, not like a catalog.

**Reading Level:** Below 8th grade. Aim for 6th or 7th.

**Avoid:**
- Cringe or hype language
- Sales language
- Exclamation points
- Colons used as sentence separators, or for emphasis in headlines
- Repeating yourself
- Em dashes. Use a period or comma instead.
- Hyphens as separators. Use a period or comma instead.
- Years in claims (like "best decks of 2026") unless the year is actually needed, like news. It ages badly.

**Dashes — when they ARE allowed:**
- Ranges and comparisons: $2-$4, 30-60 minutes, ages 25-40
- Compound words: pre-filled, well-known, long-term, evidence-based

**Text Format:**
- Prefer short sentences and short paragraphs with room to breathe. Vary the length. Don't lock into one rhythm.
- Conversational, human language, not corporate copy.
- Use lists only when a list actually helps. Don't reach for one by default.
- Use H2 and H3 for major sections.
- No orphaned headings. If a paragraph doesn't follow a heading, cut the heading.
- Mix in some longer sentences with conjunctions and subordinate clauses, but keep short ones as the backbone.

## What We Avoid Saying (brand-specific)

Pulled directly from client feedback on the site rebuild, not just general hype-avoidance:

- "Professional installation," "we install," or anything implying Brunsell installs. Brunsell supplies materials and connects customers to a contractor list. It doesn't install.
- "We design" or "fits perfectly" for millwork and stairways. Say the team builds to the customer's or contractor's drawing instead.
- "Premium," "superior grain match," or "precision match" for wood grain and color. Wood is a natural material and varies. Say the piece is individually selected instead of promising a match.
- "Custom engineering" or "holistic approach" for stairways. Brunsell builds to code and to a provided drawing. It doesn't do structural engineering.
- "Request a Quote" as the default CTA for design-adjacent services (stairways, custom millwork, decks). Use "Get in Touch" or "Contact Us" instead. A lightweight quote form is fine for simple, standard lumber orders only.
- Technical lumber jargon like S2S or S4S in general copy. Keep it in one contractor-facing FAQ entry at most. Everywhere else, describe the product in plain terms.
- Changing "88 years in business" or the founding year without a sitewide update. Keep it consistent across every page.

## What We Do Well

- Name both audiences without picking one over the other: contractors get partnership terms (dedicated account manager, volume pricing, free delivery), homeowners get plain guidance on how a project works.
- Explain a process in steps when the product has one. Wood countertops, for example: upload photos, talk to a designer, build. Keep finish names simple (bar top, oil finish for a cutting board, "the good stuff") without over-explaining the differences unless asked.
- Give a real next step instead of a generic sales prompt. "Talk to a millwork professional" beats "act now."
- Say what a page is not, when that helps. Stairways are built to a provided drawing, not designed from scratch. That's a feature, not a limitation. Say so.

## Page Structure

Most product and service pages follow this shape:

1. A short opening that says what the product is and who it's for. No "Welcome to our Custom Millwork page."
2. H2 sections for each option or step (materials, finishes, process).
3. A contractor-specific section where it applies ("Partner with Us," account benefits, one-stop-shop framing: Brunsell supplies materials, Brunsell can connect you to a contractor).
4. FAQ, phrased as real questions a customer would ask (wood species, style, timeline, whether they have inspiration photos or existing layout).
5. One clear next step. "Get in Touch" or "Contact us, talk to a millwork professional today," not a hard sales push.

## Do / Don't

**Do:**
> Every project starts with wood picked for the job. We match species and finish to what you're building, from a single mantel to a full staircase. Bring us your drawing, or your contractor's, and we'll build to it.

**Don't:**
> Brunsell Custom Millwork equals unlimited choices and spectacular results! Our team of wood experts and artisans stands ready to accentuate style, texture, and color to fulfill your needs.

---

**Do:**
> Wood countertops start with a few photos. Send us what you're picturing, talk it through with a designer, and we'll build it. Pick from three finishes: a bar top, an oil finish for a cutting board, or the good stuff.

**Don't:**
> Our wood countertop program offers unparalleled craftsmanship and a premium selection of finishes designed to transform your space.

---

**Do:**
> Brunsell doesn't install. We supply the materials and can connect you with a contractor from our list. Ask us for a recommendation.

**Don't:**
> We offer professional installation services backed by decades of holistic, custom-engineered craftsmanship.

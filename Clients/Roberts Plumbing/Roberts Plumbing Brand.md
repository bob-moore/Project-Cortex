Homeowners and business/property owners across Madison, Janesville, and Southern Wisconsin (Dane, Waukesha, Jefferson, Dodge, Milwaukee, Columbia, Washington, Racine, Kenosha, Fond du Lac, Sauk, Ozaukee, Rock, Walworth counties per current site copy — client has flagged this list as possibly too broad and wants tighter focus on Madison & Janesville specifically. - **Pain points (document-sourced, client's own words):** "drain rot, water leaking, not getting hot water" — both emergency ("problem") and informed ("solution") searchers. Common triggers: burst/frozen pipes, no hot water, clogged drains, sewer backups, low water pressure.

## 3. Brand Voice & Tone

- **Voice in 3–5 words:** Direct, trustworthy, no-nonsense, local
- **This brand sounds like:** A local, family-owned tradesperson who's confident and responsive but not salesy — short benefit-first statements, heavy on urgency for emergencies, plainspoken for planned work (remodels, installs).
- **This brand never sounds like:** Corporate, jargon-heavy, or hype-driven. No exclamation-point stacking, no vague "trusted partner" filler without a concrete reason behind it.
- **Formality / humor / sentence style / POV:** Second person ("you/your home"), contractions used freely, short paragraphs with frequent subheads and bullet lists. Little to no humor — register is competent-and-caring, not playful. Headlines are benefit-first or direct-statement, occasionally question-based ("Need a reliable plumber for your home?").
- **Sample approved copy** (from live site + agency-drafted, client-approved page copy):
  > "A failing water heater can leave you without hot water when you least expect it. Whether your unit is leaking, making noise, or simply reaching the end of its life, Robert's Plumbing provides professional installation for water softeners and treatment options to keep your water heater working properly."
  > "Homeowners throughout the Madison area trust Robert's Plumbing because we focus on quality, transparency, and long-term results."
  > "Robert's has been Amazing! We pretty much have them on speed dial! Anytime we need anything they find time to fit us in and are always kind and speedy." (customer review, robertsplumbing.us/reviews/)
- **Sample rejected copy:** Anything overly corporate ("we leverage industry-leading solutions"), anything that leads with price/discounts as the hook, anything framing them as a big regional chain rather than a local family business.

## 5. SEO Requirements

- **Keyword strategy (document-sourced, from actual agency reporting):** Core themes are emergency plumbing ("24 hour plumbing," "emergency plumber," "emergency plumber madison wi"), local + service-type combinations ("local plumbers near me," "plumbing services madison wi"), and residential/commercial split pages. Top-converting PPC keywords as of mid-2026: "plumber," "plumber near me," "plumber madison," "madison plumbing."
- **Example keywords:** water heater repair/replacement, water softener installation, commercial plumbing madison, residential plumbing services, drain cleaning/hydro jetting, backflow prevention.
- **Dominant search intent:** Mixed commercial + emergency/transactional. Organic search drives 62% of leads (as of mid-2026 reporting), paid search ~33%.
- **Domain authority context:** Organic ranking improved from page 4 (Feb 2026) to page 1-2 for core plumbing themes by mid-2026 — a fast-improving, actively-managed SEO account, not a legacy high-authority domain. First-time visitor traffic up ~29% YoY as of the Nov 2026 annual review.
- **On-page SEO rules:** New pages get submitted to Google index and old landing pages removed as part of routine work. `/landing-page` is set to `noindex` (used for PPC/referral campaigns only — real service pages should be the ones ranking organically). **Hard rule: no keyword bidding or optimization for "heating" or "cooling"** — explicitly called out twice in meeting notes (March 2026: "make sure no keyword bidding for heating OR cooling"; team also "refreshed description and services to remove heating & cooling" from GBP).

## 9. Success Metrics & Goals

- **Primary conversion preference: phone calls over web forms.** Client's own words: "prefer that users call as opposed to sending in a contact form (more women)... stressing that people should call is super important." Website success target is **fewer than 10 forms/day** (forms are treated as a secondary/backup channel, not the goal) — copy and CTAs should consistently push toward calling, not just submitting a form.
- **Revenue goal:** Grow service department from ~$1.2M (2025) toward $1.6-2M, with residential as the primary growth target and commercial as a secondary push (larger contracts).
- **Performance so far (as of the Nov 2026 annual review, document-sourced):** ~17,000 first-time visitors (+29.3% YoY), organic search driving 62% of leads (+24.3% YoY), contact forms driving 40%+ of conversions, paid search driving 33% of leads, organic ranking improved 17.4% year over year.
- **How the client measures success internally:** profit/loss for Gina and Brad directly — the agency's own service report is the secondary/agency-side measure. Worth keeping this framing in mind for any reporting-facing copy.

# Roberts Plumbing — Voice

Read by every content/copy skill before drafting for this client, alongside [[Roberts Plumbing Brand]].

## Format & Style Defaults

Agency-wide baseline (see `templates/Client Voice.md`). **One documented exception for this client** — see Client-Specific Format Notes below: light exclamation-point use, one per section at most, on CTA lines. This is a real, observed pattern, not an oversight — it overrides the default in that specific context only.

**Tone:** Simple and authentic.

**Reading Level:** Below 8th grade. Aim for 6th or 7th.

**Avoid:**
- Cringe or hype language
- Sales language
- Exclamation points (except the light CTA-line exception noted below)
- Colons used as sentence separators
- Repeating yourself
- Em dashes as separators or for emphasis. Use a period or comma instead.
- Hyphens as separators. Use a period or comma instead.
- Years in claims (like "best XXX of 2026") unless the year is actually needed, like news. It ages badly.

**Dashes — when they ARE allowed:**
- Ranges and comparisons: $2-$4, 30-60 minutes, ages 25-40
- Compound/hyphenated words: pre-filled, well-known, long-term, evidence-based

**Text Format:**
- Prefer short sentences and short paragraphs with room to breathe. Vary the length, don't lock into one rhythm.
- Conversational, human language, not corporate copy.
- Use lists only when a list actually helps. Don't reach for one by default.
- Use H2 and H3 for major sections.
- No orphaned headings. If a paragraph doesn't follow a heading, cut the heading.
- Mix in some longer sentences with conjunctions and subordinate clauses, but keep short ones as the backbone.
- Define new terms when introduced.
- Vary the mood throughout the writing.

## Client-Specific Format Notes

- **Sentence length:** Short to medium. Lead sentences in a section are often under 15 words; supporting detail runs a bit longer but rarely more than 2 sentences before breaking into a bullet list.
- **Punctuation habits:** Contractions used freely (don't, we're, you're). Light use of exclamation points — one per section at most, usually on a CTA line ("Schedule an inspection today!"), never stacked — an intentional exception to the agency default.
- **Structural habits (lists vs. prose):** Heavy bullet use. Standard service-page pattern: 1-2 sentence intro paragraph → bulleted "why choose us" list → sub-service subheads, each with its own short intro + bullet list → phone CTA repeated after nearly every section.
- **Headline style:** Benefit-first ("Full-Service Residential Plumbing Solutions") or direct-question ("Need a reliable plumber for your home?"). Avoid cute wordplay or puns.
- **What this brand avoids:** Corporate jargon ("industry-leading," "synergy," "leverage"), hype stacking, price-led hooks, anything that reads like a big regional chain instead of a family-owned local shop.

## Brand-Specific Rules

**Brand voice:** Direct, trustworthy, no-nonsense, local

**This brand sounds like:** A confident local tradesperson — competent and responsive, not playful, not corporate. Leads with the problem the reader has, then the fix, then why to trust Robert's Plumbing specifically.

**Preferred terminology:**

| Say | Not |
|-----|-----|
| Robert's Plumbing | Roberts Plumbing (drop the apostrophe only in internal/Monday references, never in client-facing copy) |
| Request Service | Contact Us / Get a Quote |
| Call now / phone number in the CTA | "Fill out our form" as the primary ask |
| water treatment, water softener, tankless | — |

**Additional rules observed from site copy / client feedback:**

- **CTAs must push toward calling, not just form submission.** The client explicitly prefers phone calls ("more women" call rather than submit forms, per their own SEO Q&A answer) and wants form volume kept under 10/day. Every CTA should surface the phone number, not just a "contact us" link.
- **Never lead with or emphasize heating/cooling/HVAC.** See [[Roberts Plumbing Brand#7. Compliance, Legal & Claims]] — this is a hard client directive, not a style preference. It's easy to drift into since "Heating" is literally in the legal business name and shows up on some existing pages (e.g. a Contact page draft headline still reads "Contact Robert's Plumbing & Heating") — check every draft for this before it ships, the same way [[Brunsell Lumber]] copy gets checked for "we install" language.
- **Keep geographic claims tight.** Existing copy lists 14 counties; the client has flagged this as possibly overstated and wants Madison + Janesville as the real focus. Don't expand the service-area list in new copy without confirming with the account team.
- **Use real technical vocabulary, not simplified substitutes** — "hydro jetting," "backflow prevention," "tankless," "sediment flush" all appear directly in client-approved copy. This isn't a jargon-heavy brand, but it doesn't shy away from correct plumbing terms either.
- **Trust language over price language.** Reviews and approved copy both lean on reliability, speed, and communication ("honest assessments, clear pricing," "always kind and speedy") rather than discounts or lowest-price claims.

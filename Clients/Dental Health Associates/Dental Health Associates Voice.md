# Dental Health Associates — Voice

Read by every content/copy skill before drafting for this client, alongside [[Dental Health Associates Brand]]. **Check which audience a piece is for before writing** — patient-facing and recruiting content share a voice but not identical goals (see Brand.md Section 2).

## Format & Style Defaults

Agency-wide baseline (see `templates/Client Voice.md`). No conflicts observed with this client's existing copy — professional tone and sub-8th-grade reading level both hold here.

**Tone:** Simple and authentic.

**Reading Level:** Below 8th grade. Aim for 6th or 7th.

**Avoid:**
- Cringe or hype language
- Sales language
- Exclamation points
- Colons used as sentence separators
- Repeating yourself
- Em dashes as separators or for emphasis. Use a period or comma instead.
- Hyphens as separators. Use a period or comma instead.
- Years in claims (like "best XXX of 2026") unless the year is actually needed, like news. It ages badly.

**Dashes — when they ARE allowed:**
- Ranges and comparisons: $2-$4, 30-60 minutes, ages 25-40
- Compound/hyphenated words: pre-filled, well-known, long-term, evidence-based

**Text Format:**
- Prefer short sentences and short paragraphs with room to breathe. Vary the length, don't lock into one rhythm.
- Conversational, human language, not corporate copy.
- Use lists only when a list actually helps. Don't reach for one by default.
- Use H2 and H3 for major sections.
- No orphaned headings. If a paragraph doesn't follow a heading, cut the heading.
- Mix in some longer sentences with conjunctions and subordinate clauses, but keep short ones as the backbone.
- Define new terms when introduced.
- Vary the mood throughout the writing.

## Client-Specific Format Notes

- **Sentence length:** Medium, complete sentences — not choppy or heavily bulleted like a home-services site. This is a professional-services brand; copy reads more like a considered paragraph than a stacked bullet list, though careers/benefits content does use bullets for scannability (401(k), PTO, etc.).
- **Punctuation habits:** Standard, professional. No exclamation-point stacking. Occasional bold/caps for emphasis on live site ("An EXCEPTIONAL PLACE TO WORK") but sparingly.
- **Structural habits (lists vs. prose):** Prose-led for patient trust-building content (about, service descriptions); bulleted for benefits/logistics (careers, insurance, locations).
- **Headline style:** Direct and warm, not question-based or hype-driven. "Family and Specialty Dental Care. Conveniently Located Throughout Madison." / "Fulfilling Careers at Dental Health Associates."
- **What this brand avoids:** Clinical jargon toward patients, corporate-chain language, over-reliance on "family" as the only hook, gimmicky recruiting hype that undersells real specifics (bonus amount, actual benefits).

## Brand-Specific Rules

**Brand voice:** Professional, warm, trustworthy, locally-rooted

**This brand sounds like:** A genuinely local, doctor-owned practice — confident, communicative, and personal, whether it's talking to a nervous new patient or a hygienist weighing job offers.

**Preferred terminology:**

| Say | Not |
|-----|-----|
| Dental Health Associates of Madison (first reference), DHA (after) | "Dental Health Associates" alone on first mention |
| doctor-owned, practicing dentists | corporate-owned, chain, DSO |
| Schedule an Appointment | Book Now / Contact Us |

**Additional rules observed from site copy / client feedback:**

- **This client has two real audiences — patients and job candidates.** Don't write generic "dental practice" copy that tries to serve both at once; know which one a page is for. Recruiting content can be more culture/benefits-forward; patient content stays trust/comfort-forward.
- **Don't lean on "family" too hard.** It's the live tagline ("YOUR FAMILY DENTIST") and it's true, but the client's own team flagged (2022) that it gets overused — vary the trust/warmth language instead of defaulting to "family" every time.
- **Doctor-ownership is a real, usable differentiator** — "100% owned by current practicing dentists," serving Madison 50+ years. Use it specifically, especially in recruiting copy where it distinguishes DHA from corporate dental chains competing for the same hygienists.
- **Recruiting copy should be specific, not just inspirational.** The real hygienist shortage means candidates have options — lead with concrete details (signing bonus amount, actual benefits, real growth paths for entry-level roles) alongside the culture messaging, not culture messaging alone.
- **HIPAA awareness extends to copy, not just forms** — avoid implying any specific patient health information will be collected or displayed in ways that aren't already vetted; flag anything patient-data-adjacent to the account team.
- **7 locations need consistent voice, not 7 different voices** — location pages should read as the same practice in different places, not independently drafted pages (this is literally the active open item on this client).

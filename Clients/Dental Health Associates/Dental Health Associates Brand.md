## 2. Target Audience

DHA has **two real target audiences that need separate treatment**, not a single blended persona — this is the single most important thing to get right for this client.

### Audience 1: Patients (families)
- **Primary audience:** Families in the Madison/Dane County area, across 7 locations (document-sourced: "Client's target audience: Families," "Geography/Target Area(s): Dane Co - Multiple locations")
- **Pain points:** Trust in a new dentist, comfort/anxiety around dental visits, wanting a practice that communicates clearly and treats them as individuals rather than a corporate chain.
- **Sophistication level:** General consumer — plain language, no clinical jargon.
- **Audience language (VOC):** Live-site copy emphasizes "personalized treatment plans," being "open and honest," a "comfortable and relaxing atmosphere," and leans on **8,600+ patient reviews** as social proof.

### Audience 2: Job candidates (recruiting) — dental hygienists especially
- **Why this matters:** Document-sourced, repeated across years of Drive notes: there's a genuine, severe local hygienist shortage ("Staffing for hygienists has had huge impact on industry" — 2025 Overview; "Only 35 [hygiene] students this last year... they already have jobs before they graduate" — 2022 recruiting notes). The live careers page now offers a **$10,000 signing bonus for dental hygienists**, confirming this is still an acute, current need, not historical.
- **Primary audience:** Dental hygienists first, then dental assistants, administrative/front-desk, and financial coordinator roles — the "easier to fill" roles that offer internal growth paths, per 2022 notes: "Entry level, dental assistants, don't have to have experience, focus here... lots of opportunities for growth within those positions, move up within the organization."
- **Pain points (job-seeker side):** Concern about workplace culture, quality of life, whether a practice feels corporate or personal. Recruiting messaging should answer "why here and not [competitor]" for people who likely have other offers, given the labor shortage gives candidates leverage.
- **Recruiting sources / geofencing targets (document-sourced):** Dental/technical college programs — Fox Valley Tech, NE Wisconsin Tech, Chippewa Valley Tech, Waukesha County Tech, Northcentral Tech, Nicolet Area Tech, Milwaukee Area Tech, Madison Area Tech.
- **Recruiting content angle notes:** "Recruitment page should mimic admissions pages at colleges." Blog ideas already scoped in Drive: interview-question guidance for candidates, "how we are different," employee case studies including a documented "boomerang employee" story (left, went elsewhere, came back) and an employee who toured multiple offices before choosing DHA.

## 3. Brand Voice & Tone

- **Voice in 3–5 words:** Professional, warm, trustworthy, locally-rooted
- **This brand sounds like:** A genuinely local, doctor-owned practice that communicates clearly and personally — confident without being corporate, on both the patient side and the recruiting side.
- **This brand never sounds like:** A dental chain / corporate healthcare brand. Never lean on generic "family" framing too heavily even though it's true and used in the tagline — 2022 client notes explicitly flag this: "Family (but can't use this too often)."
- **Formality / humor / sentence style / POV:** Professional tone (explicit client direction: "Overall campaign tone: Professional"). Second person toward patients ("your family dentist"), warm but not casual — no slang, minimal humor. Recruiting copy can be a little more energetic/aspirational ("An EXCEPTIONAL PLACE TO WORK," "Join a Practice That Cares") than patient-facing copy, but still professional register, not gimmicky.
- **Sample approved copy (patient-facing, live site):**
  > "Clearly communicating with our patients about their oral health and comfort is the foundation [of our practice]." / "100% owned by current practicing dentists and have been serving the Madison community for over 50 years."
- **Sample approved copy (recruiting, live site):**
  > "Passionate about Teamwork and Positivity" — "a passion for providing excellent patient care," a belief that "teamwork is essential," and a commitment to "a positive and supportive work environment."
- **Sample rejected copy:** Anything that reads like a national dental chain (DSO-style corporate messaging), anything overusing "family" as the hook, anything for recruiting copy that undersells compensation/culture specifics in favor of vague inspiration only.

## 5. SEO Requirements

- **Keyword strategy:** Two parallel tracks — patient-acquisition local SEO (location pages, service pages, "family dentist [Madison neighborhood]" style terms) and recruiting SEO (job-title-specific pages: "dental hygienist jobs," "dental assistant jobs," "dentist office jobs" — these have ranked #3 or better per Jan 2024 reporting).
- **Example keywords (recruiting, document-sourced, ranking well historically):** dental hygienist jobs, dental assistant jobs, dentist office jobs.
- **Dominant search intent:** Mixed — informational/local-commercial for patients, and a genuine job-search transactional intent for the recruiting track, which is unusual for this vertical and worth remembering when picking content formats.
- **Domain authority context:** Organic performance improving as of the Nov 2026 annual review — ~17,000 first-time visitors (+29.3% YoY), organic driving 62% of leads (+24.3% YoY), contact forms driving 40%+ of conversions. 2027 focus per the agency's own recommendation: keep refining core service pages, expand blog/video content for authority.
- **On-page SEO rules:** Location pages need a **consistent template/format across all 7 locations** — this is the literal open item that surfaced this client for onboarding (Monday: "Location Page Enhancement → Duplicate other location pages with the same format"). Job pages get individual optimization per role.

## 6. Content Types & Formats

- **Location pages:** 7 of them, need consistent formatting — active work item.
- **Job/career pages:** Per-role pages (hygienist, assistant, administrative, dentist) — a real, ongoing content type, not incidental. Careers page structure emphasizes: signing bonus, doctor-ownership, benefits (401(k) matching, health/dental/vision, PTO, professional development), and culture pillars.
- **Blog:** Active content type — planned/scoped topics in Drive include candidate-facing "what to ask in an interview," "how we're different," and employee case studies (boomerang employee, multi-office-tour employee).
- **Service pages:** Standard dental service content (hygiene, insurance acceptance, etc.) — "Accepted Insurance" page enhancement was an active May deliverable.

## 7. Compliance, Legal & Claims

> [!warning] HIPAA compliance is a hard technical requirement, not a style note
> DHA's forms must be HIPAA-compliant. The agency previously had to migrate from a generic form tool to **JotForm specifically because of HIPAA** requirements (documented in 2022 Drive notes — this caused real tracking/integration complications with CallRail at the time). Any new form, appointment-request flow, or patient-data-adjacent feature must account for this — flag to the developer/account team before building anything that collects patient health information.

- **Don't overuse "family" framing** — flagged explicitly by the client's own team in 2022 notes, even though "YOUR FAMILY DENTIST" is the live tagline. Use it, don't lean on it as the sole differentiator.
- **Ownership structure is a real differentiator, use it accurately:** "100% owned by current practicing dentists" — don't blur this into generic "locally owned" language; the doctor-ownership specifically matters for both patient trust and recruiting differentiation from corporate DSOs.

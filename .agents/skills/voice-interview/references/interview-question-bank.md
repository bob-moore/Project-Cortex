# Interview Question Bank

Seven categories, adapted from a personal-voice interview methodology to a
client/brand context. An interviewee is a business, not an individual — so
every category is reframed around what the brand's writing does and refuses to
do, not around personal biography. Skip any question that's already answered
by a cited example elsewhere in the interview; don't ask it twice.

Each question below is a starting point, not a script. Follow the interview
rules in `SKILL.md` Phase 1 — push back on vague answers, demand an example,
call out contradictions — regardless of which question triggered the thread.

## Quick Calibration (use for Quick-depth interviews)

Ten questions, one from most categories, chosen for highest signal per
question asked:

1. What's one thing your business believes about [their industry/service]
   that most competitors' marketing gets wrong?
2. Read me a sentence from your own site or a competitor's that makes you
   cringe. What specifically is wrong with it?
3. Show me a real sentence you've written or approved that sounds exactly
   like you. What makes it work?
4. What's a word or phrase you'd never let into your marketing copy?
5. How do you sound when you're explaining something technical to someone who
   doesn't know the field yet?
6. Do you use bullet lists and headers by default, or only when the content
   forces it?
7. What's a topic, claim, or approach you will never use in your marketing,
   even if it would convert well?
8. What's a red flag in a competitor's marketing that makes you distrust them
   immediately?
9. When something you've written gets edited by someone else, what usually
   gets changed?
10. If I showed you a draft and it just felt "off" — not wrong, just not you —
    what's the most likely reason?

## Full Bank

### 1. Positioning & Point of View (~10-12 questions)

- What does this brand believe about [industry/service] that most competitors'
  marketing doesn't say out loud?
- What's a claim you'd defend even if a customer pushed back hard on it?
- What's conventional wisdom in your industry's marketing that you think is
  wrong or overused?
- Who is this NOT for? Who reads your copy and should immediately feel it's
  not for them?
- What do you want a reader to feel in the first two sentences?
- What's the single most important thing a customer should understand about
  you that most of your marketing fails to convey?
- Is there a founding story, principle, or stance that should show up in tone
  even when it's not the topic?
- What's something true about your business that sounds like marketing spin
  but isn't — how do you write it so it doesn't sound like spin?

### 2. Writing Mechanics (~15-18 questions)

- How do you actually write — short punchy sentences, longer explanatory
  ones, or a mix? Show me an example.
- How do your pieces typically open? Show me a real opening line you like.
- How do they close? Do you summarize, call to action, or just stop?
- What's your relationship with contractions — always, never, depends on
  context?
- Do you use the Oxford comma? Do you care?
- Semicolons, em dashes, exclamation points — which do you use, which do you
  forbid?
- Do you write in first person ("we"), second person ("you"), or a mix?
- What's a word you overuse without realizing it?
- What's a word you love and use deliberately?
- What's a word or phrase you'd never use? Why specifically — what does it
  signal that you don't want signaled?
- How long do your paragraphs run before you break them up?
- Do you define jargon when you use it, or assume the reader already knows
  it?
- Show me a sentence you've written that you're proud of. What makes it work
  mechanically — not just "it's good," but what's the actual sentence doing?

### 3. Aesthetic Crimes (~10 questions)

- What phrase, if you saw it in your own copy, would make you ask "who wrote
  this"?
- What's a marketing cliché in your specific industry that everyone uses and
  you refuse to?
- Show me a real sentence — yours or a competitor's — that's trying too hard.
  What's wrong with it?
- What kind of content do you consider lazy or phoned-in? What does lazy look
  like specifically, versus content that's just simple?
- Is there a format (listicle, "top 10", FAQ-stuffed page) you find yourself
  avoiding even when it would perform well? Why?
- What's the difference, for you, between confident and arrogant in writing?
  Show me an example of each.

### 4. Tone & Personality Across Contexts (~12 questions)

- How do you sound when you're explaining something technical to a beginner?
- How do you sound when you're handling a complaint or a bad review response?
- How do you sound when something goes genuinely well — do you celebrate, or
  stay understated?
- Do you use humor? Where does it show up, and where would it be wrong?
- How do you disagree with a common opinion in your field without sounding
  combative?
- What does "serious" sound like for this brand versus "casual"? Is there a
  hard line between them, or does it flex by channel (email vs. website vs.
  social)?
- If a competitor makes a claim you think is misleading, how do you address
  it — direct callout, indirect contrast, or you don't engage at all?

### 5. Structural Preferences (~8 questions)

- Lists and bullets — used freely, or only when the content genuinely needs
  structure?
- Headers — do you write them as questions, statements, or keywords?
- How do you transition between ideas — explicit signposting ("Next, ...") or
  let the content carry it?
- What's your default structure for a landing page, an email, and a blog post
  — do they differ, or is there one house pattern?
- Where does the call to action go, and how direct is it?

### 6. Hard Refusals (~8 questions)

- What topic would you never write about, even if it would drive traffic or
  engagement?
- What claim would you never make, even if a competitor makes it and gets
  away with it?
- Is there a persuasion technique — urgency, fear, fake scarcity — you refuse
  to use? Where's the line between the version you'd use and the version you
  wouldn't?
- What would genuinely embarrass this brand if it appeared under their name?
- Are there compliance, legal, or factual lines that constrain wording, not
  just tone (e.g., regulated claims, licensing language, guarantees)?

### 7. Red Flags & Trust Signals (~8 questions)

- What's a pattern in competitor marketing that makes you immediately
  distrust them?
- What makes your audience specifically distrust marketing copy in your
  industry?
- What's a signal — a phrase, a claim, a format — that tells you someone
  doesn't actually know what they're talking about?
- What builds trust with your audience fastest? Is it specificity, proof,
  restraint, something else?
- What's the fastest way a piece of writing loses your audience's attention
  or credibility?

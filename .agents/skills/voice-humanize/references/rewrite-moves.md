# Rewrite Moves

Use these moves to make writing sound more natural without changing meaning.

## Replace Inflated Language

```text
Before: This initiative serves as a pivotal step in our broader transformation.
After: This is the next step in the work.
```

Use a plainer sentence when the original adds importance without information.

## Restore Normal Verbs

```text
Before: The platform enables teams to leverage shared insights.
After: Teams can use shared insights.
```

Simple verbs are often clearer. Keep technical verbs when they are precise.

## Cut False Transitions

```text
Before: Additionally, this helps teams respond faster.
After: This also helps teams respond faster.
```

Delete the transition entirely when the relationship is obvious.

## Break Stacked Clauses

```text
Before: The tool centralizes requests, enabling faster review, reducing manual
handoffs, and improving visibility.
After: The tool centralizes requests. Review moves faster, handoffs drop, and
the team can see what is waiting.
```

Split only when the sentence feels overloaded. Keep useful parallelism.

## Keep Productive Imperfection

Natural writing can include:

- A short sentence after a longer one.
- A direct aside.
- A repeated word when repetition is the clearest choice.
- A caveat that sounds like judgment instead of boilerplate.

Do not add typos, awkwardness, unsupported opinions, or fake personal stories.

## Add Specificity Safely

Add detail only from source context.

```text
Allowed: Replace "the team" with "the support team" when the source names the
support team.
Not allowed: Add "saves 3 hours a week" without evidence.
```

## Preserve The Register

Human does not always mean casual.

- Formal legal copy can still sound natural.
- Technical documentation can still use precise terms.
- Executive writing can stay restrained.
- Local service copy can be direct and plain.

Match the target voice instead of applying a universal tone.

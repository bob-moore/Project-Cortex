# Page Types

Use these structures as starting points. Adjust to the brief, brand, audience,
and available proof.

## Homepage

Purpose: orient, establish fit, and route readers.

Common structure:

1. Hero: what it is, who it is for, why it matters, primary CTA.
2. Audience or problem fit.
3. Core services, products, or paths.
4. Proof: examples, clients, testimonials, credentials, or numbers.
5. Process or differentiator.
6. Secondary paths.
7. Final CTA.

## About Page

Purpose: build trust and context.

Common structure:

1. Human or organizational reason for existing.
2. Who the work serves.
3. Beliefs, standards, or operating philosophy.
4. Relevant history.
5. Team, founder, credentials, or credibility.
6. Next step.

Avoid turning the about page into a generic sales page. It can support
conversion, but trust is the main job.

## Service Page

Purpose: explain the service and help the right reader take action.

Common structure:

1. Service outcome and fit.
2. Problem or situation.
3. What the service includes.
4. How the process works.
5. Proof or examples.
6. Who it is and is not for.
7. FAQ or objections when needed.
8. CTA.

## Product Page

Purpose: help the reader understand the product and evaluate purchase or trial.

Common structure:

1. Product promise.
2. Key use case.
3. Benefits.
4. Features as mechanisms.
5. Proof.
6. Pricing or buying path when requested.
7. CTA.

## Landing Page

Purpose: convert one audience around one offer.

Common structure:

1. Offer or outcome.
2. Audience and pain.
3. Mechanism or solution.
4. Benefits.
5. Proof.
6. Objections.
7. Risk reversal.
8. CTA.

## Sales Page

Purpose: persuade a reader to buy, book, register, or commit.

Common structure:

1. Specific promise.
2. Reader problem and cost.
3. Offer.
4. What is included.
5. Proof.
6. Objections.
7. Price, guarantee, or risk reversal when approved.
8. CTA.

Sales pages require stronger proof discipline because they carry stronger claims.

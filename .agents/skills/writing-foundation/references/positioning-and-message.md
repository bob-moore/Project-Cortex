# Positioning And Message

Use this reference for value propositions, homepage copy, landing-page copy,
campaign narratives, product messaging, offer language, and strategic rewrites.

## Positioning Frame

Complete the frame before drafting persuasive copy.

```text
For:
Who struggle with:
And currently use:
This offers:
So they can:
Unlike:
Because:
Proof:
Next action:
```

The frame is internal scaffolding. Do not force it into the final copy unless
the format calls for it.

## Message Hierarchy

Lead with the thing the reader cares about most.

1. Outcome: the concrete result or relief.
2. Benefit: why the result matters.
3. Mechanism: how the offer creates the result.
4. Feature: what the product, service, process, or idea includes.
5. Proof: why the reader should believe it.
6. Action: what to do next.

Avoid opening with company history, feature lists, process detail, or internal
language unless the reader already asked for those details.

## Audience Awareness

Choose the lead based on what the reader already knows.

| Awareness | Lead With | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unaware | recognizable situation or symptom | heavy product language |
| Problem-aware | pain, cost, frustration, or risk | premature feature detail |
| Solution-aware | desired outcome and mechanism | generic education |
| Product-aware | differentiation and proof | category explanation |
| Most-aware | offer, urgency, risk reversal, action | long setup |

## Promise Rules

Make the promise specific enough to understand and evaluate.

Prefer:

- Concrete outcome over vague improvement.
- Named audience over broad market.
- Verifiable proof over unsupported superiority.
- Plain mechanism over mysterious claims.
- One primary promise over a pile of benefits.

Avoid:

- "Best", "leading", "world-class", or "revolutionary" without proof.
- Time, money, ranking, health, legal, or performance claims without evidence.
- Guarantees when the operator has not approved them.

## Hero And Lead Copy

For page openings and high-stakes intros, test whether a reader can answer three
questions quickly:

- What is this?
- Who is it for?
- Why care now?

Useful structures:

```text
[Specific audience] can [desired outcome] without [pain or tradeoff].
```

```text
Stop [costly behavior]. Start [better outcome].
```

```text
The [clear category] for [specific audience] who need [specific outcome].
```

Prefer clarity first. Add style after the message can survive a quick scan.

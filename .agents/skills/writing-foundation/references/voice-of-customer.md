# Voice Of Customer

Use this reference when audience language, objections, pain points, or proof
need to be grounded in real inputs.

## Source Types

Prefer source-backed language in this order:

1. User interviews and sales calls.
2. Support tickets, chat logs, form submissions, reviews, and survey responses.
3. Testimonials and case studies.
4. Existing client-approved copy and brand notes.
5. Competitor reviews and public market language.
6. Operator-provided description.
7. Judged assumptions.

Do not present inferred or operator-supplied language as customer research.
Label it accurately.

## Mining Targets

Extract phrases that reveal:

- Trigger moments: what happened before the search or purchase.
- Pain language: how the audience describes the problem.
- Desired outcomes: what success sounds like in the audience's words.
- Alternatives: tools, habits, providers, or workarounds already used.
- Objections: price, trust, effort, switching cost, timing, risk.
- Proof points: specific results, before/after descriptions, named wins.
- Vocabulary: terms to use, terms to avoid, category language.

## Evidence Labels

Use consistent labels in notes and handoffs.

- `source-backed`: linked, cited, or traceable to a source.
- `user-provided`: supplied by the operator or project notes.
- `measured`: observed in analytics, tooling, or other data.
- `judged`: editorial judgment.
- `assumed`: necessary but unverified.

## Safe Extraction

Quote short phrases only when useful. Paraphrase long source passages. Preserve
meaning and do not manufacture specificity.

Good extraction:

```text
Source phrase: "I spend half my Monday chasing status updates."
Usable copy angle: Stop losing Monday to status updates.
Label: source-backed
```

Unsafe extraction:

```text
Unsupported claim: Teams save 50% of their Monday workload.
Issue: invented metric.
```

## Objection Handling

Turn objections into useful copy only when the answer is known.

```text
Objection: "This will take too long to set up."
Copy need: setup time, onboarding support, migration path, or proof.
Evidence needed: measured setup time or approved process detail.
```

If the answer is unknown, list the objection as an open question instead of
answering it with invented confidence.

# Design Inspiration Library Research

## Bottom line

Do not build one large inspiration gallery. Build a routed reference system with separate lanes:

1. Local-business realism and copyable starters.
2. PPC and conversion patterns.
3. Editorial and visual-quality references.
4. Copy, proof, and messaging references.
5. Implementation resources for the approved stack.
6. Reverse-engineering and design-rule extraction.

The first two lanes should be the default for ProjectCortex. The third lane should stretch taste without becoming the default style source. The fifth lane belongs downstream of an approved design direction.

The user's observation is correct: most modern design galleries overrepresent SaaS, developer tools, AI products, and venture-backed startups. That is not an adequate visual or content model for HVAC, legal, mortgage, dental, home services, local professional services, or other PPC clients.

## Recommended starting set

### Default visual and structural references

#### One Page Love

URL: https://onepagelove.com/

What it provides:

- Curated one-page websites.
- Whole-page references and section-level references.
- Categories such as Landing Page, Service, Physical Product, Informational, and Photography.
- Templates and tools alongside inspiration.

Why it belongs:

- More useful for one-page campaign work than broad SaaS-product galleries.
- Section-level browsing maps well to hero, proof strip, service explanation, FAQ, and CTA research.

Limitations:

- Still contains many product and design-oriented examples.
- Inspiration is not conversion evidence.
- Copy and assets must be treated as reference, not copied verbatim.

#### SiteInspire

URL: https://www.siteinspire.com/

Useful paths:

- https://www.siteinspire.com/websites
- https://www.siteinspire.com/websites?categories=industry%3Aservices

What it provides:

- Curated website showcase with filters for categories, styles, types, subjects, and platforms.
- Service-oriented filtering is more useful for local-business research than a SaaS-only gallery.

Why it belongs:

- Stronger visual breadth than typical landing-page SaaS libraries.
- Useful for finding editorial, hospitality, professional-service, and agency work.

Limitations:

- High-end showcase work can be too art-directed for a local lead-generation site.
- Not generally copyable as a production template.

#### Awwwards service directory

URL: https://www.awwwards.com/websites/services/

What it provides:

- Service-category website nominees and award-level references.

Why it belongs:

- Good stretch source for composition, typography, photography, and brand confidence.

Limitations:

- Award criteria favor novelty and visual impact, not necessarily calls, forms, local SEO, accessibility, or PPC conversion.
- Use as a stretch reference only.

### Copyable or implementation-adjacent local-business sources

#### Astra Website Templates

URL: https://wpastra.com/website-templates/

What it provides:

- Large WordPress starter-template library with free and paid designs.
- Practical business and professional-service starting points.

Why it belongs:

- Better fit for the WordPress delivery lane than abstract inspiration galleries.
- Useful for studying realistic navigation, service pages, testimonials, contact flows, and local-business content density.

Limitations:

- Builder/theme ecosystem creates design and implementation coupling.
- Quality and visual distinctiveness vary.
- Use as a structural starting point, not as the agency's permanent design language.

#### Squarespace Professional Services templates

URL: https://www.squarespace.com/templates/browse/topic/professional-services?vertical=DOMESTIC_SERVICES

What it provides:

- Curated service-business and professional-services templates.

Why it belongs:

- More restrained and brand-oriented than many SaaS template libraries.
- Useful for local professional services, consultants, home services, and small firms.

Limitations:

- Platform-specific and less modular than a design reference system.
- Copyability is primarily within Squarespace.

#### Webflow Templates

URL: https://webflow.com/templates

Useful category visible on the current templates page:

- Professional Services.

What it provides:

- Free and paid HTML/Webflow templates, including one-page, multi-page, agency, real-estate, and professional-service patterns.

Why it belongs:

- More implementation-realistic than a screenshot-only gallery.
- Paid templates can provide a complete starting structure when the stack and license fit.

Limitations:

- Marketplace quality and style vary widely.
- Many templates still converge on contemporary startup aesthetics.
- Treat licenses and CMS/platform lock-in as explicit metadata.

#### Elementor Website Kits

URL: https://elementor.com/library/website-kits/

What it provides:

- WordPress website-building and kit ecosystem aimed at professional creation and agencies.

Why it belongs:

- Relevant for WordPress client delivery and local-service page structures.
- Useful for implementation references after the visual direction is chosen.

Limitations:

- The current public page is product-led rather than a clean local-business catalog.
- Builder dependency and ecosystem lock-in are significant.

## PPC and landing-page research sources

### Unbounce landing-page examples

URL: https://unbounce.com/landing-page-examples/

What it provides:

- Curated landing-page examples with commentary around conversion performance.
- Dedicated lead-generation and campaign-strategy material.
- Industry examples such as insurance and other lead-gen categories.

Why it belongs:

- Strongest of the reviewed sources for connecting page structure to conversion intent.
- Useful for CTA hierarchy, forms, message matching, proof, and above-the-fold decisions.

Limitations:

- Noticeable B2B and SaaS bias.
- Vendor content naturally favors Unbounce-style workflows.
- Use the conversion principles, not the visual default.

### Landingi templates

URL: https://landingi.com/templates/

What it provides:

- Professional customizable landing-page and popup templates.
- Campaign-oriented structures rather than general website portfolios.

Why it belongs:

- Good source for lead-generation page anatomy, forms, campaign-specific pages, and rapid test variants.

Limitations:

- Platform-centric.
- Template quality and originality vary.
- Requires a conversion brief before use to avoid formulaic pages.

### Leadpages templates

URL: https://www.leadpages.com/templates

What it provides:

- Landing-page templates intended for lead capture and campaign use.

Why it belongs:

- Useful for comparing lead-gen page structures, CTA placement, form patterns, and mobile layout.

Limitations:

- Strong platform and marketing-template bias.
- Better as a structural reference than as a visual-quality benchmark.

### Landingfolio

URL: https://www.landingfolio.com/

What it provides:

- Landing-page inspiration, page sections, components, templates, and component libraries for Tailwind, Webflow, and Figma.
- Searchable industry and color filters.

Why it belongs:

- Very useful for section-level research and implementation handoff.
- Its separation of inspiration, components, and templates is close to the system we need.

Limitations:

- Current taxonomy is heavily SaaS/product-led; SaaS is the largest visible category.
- Should be a secondary source for local-business work.
- Component libraries can reintroduce generic startup aesthetics if used before the brief.

### GoodUI

URL: https://goodui.org/

What it provides:

- A/B-tested and conversion-oriented UI patterns, lessons, and experiments.

Why it belongs:

- Supplies evidence-oriented CRO patterns rather than only screenshots.
- Useful for form length, CTA treatment, trust, pricing/offer framing, and test hypotheses.

Limitations:

- Not a local-business visual library.
- Patterns must be treated as hypotheses, not universal laws.
- Some material is gated or presented as marketing content.

### Google Ads Quality Score guidance

URL: https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118

What it provides:

- First-party guidance on ad relevance and landing-page experience as part of Search campaign quality.

Why it belongs:

- Keeps PPC landing-page design connected to message match, relevance, and user experience rather than aesthetics alone.

Limitations:

- It is guidance, not a design gallery or page template source.
- It does not replace conversion research or client-specific offer strategy.

## Useful secondary stretch sources

### Refero Styles

URL: https://styles.refero.design/

Use for DESIGN.md-style visual rule extraction and curated style directions. Keep as a reference input, not a default local-business source.

### awesome-design-md

URL: https://github.com/voltagent/awesome-design-md

Use for studying portable design contracts, tokens, typography, layout grammar, and anti-pattern documentation. Do not copy brand-specific DESIGN.md files as project authority.

### Typewolf

URL: https://www.typewolf.com/

Use for typography and type-pairing research when a local brand needs a more distinctive voice. It is a typography reference, not a landing-page source.

### Hoverstat

URL: https://www.hoverstat.es/

Use for interaction and motion references. Keep motion downstream of hierarchy, accessibility, and reduced-motion requirements.

### MotionSites

URL: https://motionsites.ai/

Use as a motion/prompt reference only. It is not a reliable source for local-business conversion structure.

### Aceternity UI

URL: https://ui.aceternity.com/

Use downstream for implementation patterns when the stack and visual direction justify it. Do not use it to define the design direction; its visual language can make sites look like generic modern SaaS products.

### UI UX Pro Max

URL: https://github.com/nextlevelbuilder/ui-ux-pro-max-skill

Use as a possible future second-opinion rule/checklist source. Do not install wholesale until its unique methods are compared with the existing ProjectCortex design discipline, taste skills, accessibility guidance, and rubric.

## Reference taxonomy for ProjectCortex

Every saved reference should be tagged across these dimensions:

- `source_type`: local-site, landing-page, section, template, CRO-pattern, copy, typography, motion, component, design-contract, reverse-engineering
- `industry`: home-services, legal, mortgage, dental, healthcare, medspa, insurance, accounting, real-estate, hospitality, restaurant, fitness, education, nonprofit, professional-services, other
- `funnel_stage`: awareness, consideration, quote, booking, emergency-call, consultation, ecommerce, retention
- `page_archetype`: service-home, location-page, service-detail, PPC-lead, quote-form, booking, consultation, campaign-comparison, case-study, local-retail
- `primary_conversion`: phone-call, form-submit, booking, estimate-request, store-visit, purchase, email-signup
- `proof_pattern`: reviews, credentials, guarantees, before-after, case-study, local-presence, partners, pricing, availability
- `visual_traits`: editorial, utilitarian, premium, warm, local, industrial, clinical, playful, restrained, high-contrast, photographic, typographic
- `stack`: WordPress, Elementor, Astra, Kadence, Webflow, Squarespace, static, unknown
- `copyability`: screenshot-only, structural-reference, template-import, code-copy, licensed-template
- `saas_bias`: low, medium, high
- `license_status`: inspect, permissive, marketplace-license, platform-bound, unknown

A useful reference record should include:

- URL and capture date
- screenshot or page image
- why it was saved
- what is worth borrowing structurally
- what must not be copied
- headline/CTA/proof observations
- mobile behavior
- accessibility or performance observations
- client-industry and funnel tags
- license/copyability notes

## Anti-generic collection rule

For a new client or campaign, collect a small intentional set rather than searching indefinitely:

- 3 references from the client's industry or adjacent local industries
- 2 PPC/conversion references for the funnel goal
- 1 typography or editorial stretch reference
- 1 implementation reference from the target stack

Then produce a short direction card explaining:

- which qualities are being borrowed
- which SaaS conventions are being rejected
- how the direction supports the offer and audience
- which content/proof assumptions remain unresolved

Do not let an agent choose a visual style from a gallery before it has a brief, offer, audience, primary conversion, and proof inventory.

## Recommended next step

Do not install another large design library yet. Create a small project-local reference index with the taxonomy above and manually seed it with:

- One Page Love
- SiteInspire services
- Astra or the actual WordPress starter system used for the client
- Unbounce lead-generation examples
- Landingi or Leadpages campaign templates
- GoodUI conversion patterns
- Google Ads landing-page relevance guidance
- Refero Styles as a controlled stretch source

After several real projects, add sources only when a gap is observed. The future `design-prototype` workflow should query this index by industry, funnel, archetype, and visual trait rather than present a flat gallery.

## Access notes

- Siteinspire and One Page Love are accessible reference sources but may use sign-in, subscription, or collection features for personal organization.
- Land-book and Lapa Ninja were blocked by Cloudflare during this research session; do not make them dependencies.
- Marketplace and template resources require per-template license review.
- “Copyable” should mean reusable structure or licensed template/code, not verbatim copying of another brand's copy, images, or identity.

Copy drafted by the `web-copywriter` skill (2026-07-15) for the items on [[People4Pulse#Open Items]] that needed real drafting, not just implementation of client-provided wording. Read [[People4Pulse Brand]] and [[People4Pulse Voice]] for the rules this was drafted against.

## 1. "Overdose Crisis" Homepage Section — 3 Options

Replaces the flagged "rising deaths statewide" placeholder. All three apply the bipartisan-framing rule ([[People4Pulse Brand]] Section 7) and are drafted so any of the three stat sets can swap in — **but the final stat set is still unconfirmed by Alan, don't ship until he picks one.**

> [!warning] Don't ship without Alan's stat confirmation
> This is a live compliance flag, not just a copy preference — see [[People4Pulse Brand]] Section 9.

### Option A — "Still Too High" (urgency, cumulative scale)

**Headline:** 12,579 Wisconsinites have died from drug overdoses since 2016.

**Body:** Overdose deaths remain unacceptably high in Wisconsin. Opioids account for the largest share of that toll, and this crisis reaches every family in the state, regardless of party.

*Source: Wisconsin Department of Health Services*

### Option B — "Harm Reduction Is Working" (trend, progress framing)

**Headline:** Opioid overdose deaths in Wisconsin dropped from 1,422 to 817 in a single year.

**Body:** Total drug overdose deaths fell too, from 1,793 to 1,261. That progress is real, but it doesn't erase the toll: 12,579 people have died from drug overdoses statewide since 2016.

*Source: Wisconsin Department of Health Services*

### Option C — "Shared Crisis" (leads with Alan's own framing, minimal stats) — recommended

**Headline:** Overdose deaths remain unacceptably high in Wisconsin.

**Body:** This isn't a Democratic problem or a Republican one. Every community in the state has lost someone to overdose or to a system that punishes drug use instead of treating it. Wisconsin recorded 1,261 drug overdose deaths last year alone.

*Source: Wisconsin Department of Health Services*

**Writer's recommendation:** Option C reads closest to Alan's own words from the 2026-07-07 meeting and is the safest bipartisan framing since it doesn't lean on any single number set that's still under debate. Option B is strongest if the client ultimately wants a "harm reduction is working" narrative, but commits to year-over-year figures now. Option A is the most emotionally weighted — best for arguing scale over time, at the cost of not showing progress.

## 2. PULSE Definition Blurb — About Page

Suggested placement: near the top of About, ahead of or alongside "Who We Are" — this was entirely missing from the rebuild, so it should be one of the first things a visitor sees on that page, not buried under the 8 values.

> PULSE stands for Promoting Understanding, Lifting Stigma, & Empowerment. It's not just where our name comes from — it's what we do. Every meeting, every resource, and every conversation we host works toward those three things: building real understanding of what substance use actually looks like, lifting the stigma that keeps people from asking for help, and empowering people who use drugs to lead the fight for their own dignity and autonomy.
